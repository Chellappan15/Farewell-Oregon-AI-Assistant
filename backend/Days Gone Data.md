# 1. How Long Is Days Gone

## 1.1 Main Story Completion Time

A first-time playthrough focused on the main story generally takes **35–45 hours**. Familiar players who skip optional content and minimize travel can finish faster. Mission length varies considerably: a short dialogue ride can take minutes, while a mandatory horde fight may require multiple attempts.

## 1.2 Main Story and Side Content

Allow **45–65 hours** for the campaign with a substantial number of camp jobs, NERO checkpoints, ambush camps, and infestations. Camp jobs often appear between main missions; accepting them as they arrive avoids excessive backtracking.

## 1.3 Completionist Run

A broad **60–90+ hour** estimate is reasonable for clearing storylines, hordes, map activities, and collectibles. A strict personal definition of 100% may take longer if it includes all optional collectibles, trophy lists, challenges, and difficulty runs. The in-game storyline percentage, the PlayStation platinum trophy, and literal collection of every collectible are **different completion targets**.

## 1.4 New Game+

New Game+ allows another campaign playthrough while retaining much of the character's earned progression and equipment. A focused repeat campaign can take approximately **25–40 hours**, depending on difficulty, familiarity, and optional activities. New Game+ is not a substitute for clearing the original playthrough's outstanding collectibles and activities.

## 1.5 Survival and Survival II Modes

Survival removes or restricts conveniences such as conventional fast travel and much of the HUD, making navigation and engagements slower. Survival II adds higher difficulty. A first Survival run may take **40–60+ hours**; these are estimates rather than fixed or official completion times. Plan extra travel time and use environmental landmarks.

# 2. Tips and Tricks

## 2.1 Survival Fundamentals

### 2.1.1 Understand the three survival meters

- **Health:** Damage tolerance; replenish with bandages, medkits, and other healing items.
- **Stamina:** Used for sprinting and rolling. Exhaustion during a horde chase can be fatal.
- **Focus:** Slows perceived time while aiming after the relevant skill is acquired. It is especially useful for precision shots and suppressing approaching Freakers.

### 2.1.2 Use sound deliberately

Gunfire, explosions, alarms, and bike noise can attract nearby Freakers. Suppressors and stealth kills help when clearing isolated targets. Loud combat is often appropriate when you have a prepared escape route, a defensible position, or a planned explosive chain.

### 2.1.3 Watch the day–night cycle

Freakers are more active in the open at night, while many hordes shelter in caves or structures during the day. Weather, time, and nearby activity can change encounters. Scout an area before committing to a fight.

## 2.2 Health, Stamina and Focus

### 2.2.1 NERO injector priorities

Each injector lets you increase **Health, Stamina, or Focus**. There is no universally optimal allocation, but a beginner-friendly order is:

1. **Stamina early:** Extra sprinting and dodge capacity improves survival against groups.
2. **Focus next:** Supports accurate headshots and sustained automatic-weapon fire.
3. **Health as needed:** Particularly valuable for players taking frequent hits or tackling tougher enemies.

A balanced approach is safer than neglecting one attribute entirely. Collect injectors whenever you can safely reach a checkpoint or research site.

### 2.2.2 Skill-tree strategy

- **Melee:** Improve durability, efficiency, and damage for conserving ammunition against lone enemies.
- **Ranged:** Improve accuracy, recoil control, ammunition handling, and Focus efficiency.
- **Survival:** Improve resource visibility, gathering, tracking, and practical endurance.

**Important:** The **Focus Shot** skill unlocks Focus aiming; upgrading the Focus meter alone does not replace learning the skill.

### 2.2.3 Skill priorities for stealth and carrying capacity

Focused Shot is an early Ranged skill that briefly slows time while aiming, making headshots easier. Nocked Up improves crossbow damage and is particularly valuable for players using crossbows as their main stealth weapon. Ear to the Ground expands the usefulness of Survival Vision by revealing enemies. Carry That Weight increases material and throwable carrying capacity and becomes especially valuable during extended horde fights. Field Repairs is an early priority for players who rely on melee weapons because it prevents loss of a strong weapon when durability becomes low.

## 2.3 Stealth and Combat

### 2.3.1 Stealth procedure

1. Park the bike outside enemy detection range.
2. Mark enemies with binoculars before entering.
3. Use tall grass, cover, and crouch movement.
4. Isolate enemies with rocks when practical.
5. Use stealth takedowns and suppressed shots against isolated targets.
6. Move bodies only when the encounter mechanics and positioning allow safe concealment; otherwise change routes before another patrol arrives.

### 2.3.2 Human enemies

Prioritize snipers, machine gunners, and enemies with a clear line of sight. Use cover rather than standing in open ground. Flank carefully: camps can contain tripwires, bear traps, and reinforcements.

### 2.3.3 Freakers

- **Swarmers:** Headshots and controlled melee attacks work on isolated targets.
- **Newts:** Check rooftops and ledges; avoid lingering beneath groups.
- **Breakers:** Use distance, traps, heavy weapons, and Focus.
- **Screamers:** Eliminate quickly before they attract additional Freakers.
- **Reachers:** Prepare for fast movement and sudden lunges.
- **Hordes:** Never treat a horde like a series of independent one-on-one fights.

### 2.4.2 Resource rules

Do not craft a full inventory before searching a resource-rich area if you are likely to find ready-made supplies. Maintain a healing reserve and save rare explosive components for hordes. Recipes are unlocked through progression and activities, notably ambush-camp bunker maps; not every recipe is available at the start.

## 2.5 Motorcycle Maintenance

### 2.5.1 Fuel

The bike consumes fuel while riding. Refuel at safe fuel pumps and with gas cans found at camps, tow trucks, and various roadside locations. Many cans can be reused after leaving and returning. Avoid beginning a long cross-region trip with a nearly empty tank.

### 2.5.2 Repairs

Use scrap to repair the motorcycle in the field or pay a camp mechanic. Save some scrap for melee-weapon repairs if you rely on crafted melee weapons. Check durability after crashes, gunfire, and encounters with obstacles.

### 2.5.3 Upgrade priorities

1. **Fuel tank:** More range and fewer dangerous stops.
2. **Engine:** Better acceleration and climbing.
3. **Frame:** Increased damage tolerance.
4. **Tires and suspension:** More reliable control over rough terrain.
5. **Saddlebags:** Carry additional ammunition refills.
6. **Nitrous:** Helps with jumps, chases, and certain research-site approaches.

Availability differs by camp and trust level; not all mechanics sell the same equipment.

## 2.6 Camp Trust and Credits

### 2.6.1 Two separate economies

**Trust** unlocks merchandise and upgrades. **Credits** pay for purchases. Both are associated with individual camps rather than being one fully shared pool.

### 2.6.2 Ways to increase trust

- Complete jobs issued by that camp.
- Send rescued survivors to the camp when the game offers that choice.
- Turn in Freaker ears at the bounty collector.
- Sell collected plants and meat to the camp kitchen.
- Complete eligible story missions and nearby objectives that award camp trust.

### 2.6.3 Which camps to prioritize

- **Copeland's Camp:** Early bike upgrades.
- **Hot Springs:** Early firearm access.
- **Lost Lake:** Strong mid-game weapons and bike improvements.
- **Diamond Lake:** Valuable late-game motorcycle upgrades.
- **Wizard Island:** Powerful late-game firearms.

Before turning in a large stack of ears or food, check which camp is closest to its next trust threshold.

## 2.7 Horde Preparation

### 2.7.1 Before engaging

1. Identify the horde's cave, building, feeding ground, or watering route.
2. Scout escape paths and nearby environmental explosives.
3. Fill ammunition and saddlebags; equip suitable automatic weapons.
4. Carry attractors, explosives, and healing supplies where available.
5. Park the motorcycle facing away from the encounter with an unobstructed exit.
6. Start from cover, ideally with an explosive attack against clustered enemies.

### 2.7.2 During combat

Move continuously, funnel the horde through narrow paths, and use Focus to stabilize bursts. Throw attractors to group enemies before detonating explosives. Avoid dead ends and steep obstacles that block your escape. A failed engagement is a reason to reposition, not necessarily to keep firing until every round is gone.

### 2.7.3 Early hordes versus late hordes

Small northern hordes can be cleared earlier with careful preparation. Large late-game hordes are significantly easier with automatic weapons, more stamina, additional ammunition, and stronger explosives. Several large encounters are tied to story progression.

## 2.8 Exploration and Fast Travel

Fast travel is limited by fuel, route safety, unlocked destinations, and difficulty. Clearing infestation zones can reopen routes that would otherwise be blocked. Ambush camps reveal useful map information and crafting recipes. NERO checkpoints provide fast-travel destinations in modes that allow fast travel.

## 2.9 Progression Priorities

### 2.9.1 Early game

- Unlock Focus Shot and build a stamina reserve.
- Clear nearby ambush camps for recipes and map information.
- Repair and refuel the bike before remote missions.
- Do camp jobs for credits and trust.
- Avoid unnecessary fights with very large hordes.

### 2.9.2 Mid game

- Expand ammo capacity with saddlebags.
- Improve automatic-weapon handling and Focus.
- Clear NERO checkpoints and research sites.
- Accumulate ingredients for explosives and attractors.
- Continue camp jobs before major region transitions.

### 2.9.3 Late game

- Upgrade high-capacity firearms and the bike.
- Clear remaining hordes by region.
- Revisit previously inaccessible research sites.
- Finish storyline and collectible checklists after the campaign.

## 2.10 Mistakes to Avoid

- Spending all credits without saving for fuel, repairs, and ammunition.
- Using explosives on lone enemies when a horde fight is imminent.
- Leaving a NERO checkpoint's speakers powered before starting its generator.
- Attempting a large horde without a clear retreat path.
- Confusing a camp's trust level with available credits.
- Assuming every story mission or horde is available from the start.
- Assuming a platinum trophy automatically means every in-game collectible has been found.

# 3. Main Story Walkthrough

## 3.1 Structure and Mission Tracking

**Days Gone** uses interwoven **storylines**, not a simple numbered campaign quest chain. A mission can advance more than one storyline, and certain missions appear only after a radio call, travel, sleep, or another story trigger. The chapters below present a **narrative route** rather than falsely claiming every mission has one fixed universal order. Exact quest titles are included where reliably identifiable; narrative beats are not presented as invented mission names.

## 3.2 Prologue — Farewell and the Outbreak

### 3.2.1 Outbreak sequence

**Location:** Farewell, Oregon, during the initial disaster.

**Characters:** Deacon St. John, Sarah Whitaker, William “Boozer” Gray, and NERO personnel.

**Objectives:** Follow the evacuation sequence, protect Sarah, and establish why Deacon and Boozer remain behind. The prologue establishes Deacon's separation from Sarah and his belief that she died after evacuation.

**Critical tip:** This portion is largely scripted. Follow on-screen prompts rather than searching for optional open-world objectives.

### 3.2.2 Two years later

Deacon and Boozer survive as drifters, riding through the post-apocalyptic Pacific Northwest. Early events introduce the motorcycle, resource scavenging, melee combat, tracking, and the danger of Freaker-infested territory.

## 3.3 Northern Regions — Cascades and Belknap

### 3.3.1 Drifters on the Mountain

**Region:** Cascades.

**Main purpose:** Establish Deacon and Boozer's current situation and introduce the opening pursuit and combat systems.

**Tips:** Practice aiming, cover, and melee timing. Loot nearby supplies before leaving combat areas.

### 3.3.2 You Got a Death Wish

**Region:** Cascades.

**Main purpose:** Recover Deacon's bike and learn the importance of its parts, fuel, and repair systems.

**Tips:** Follow the bike-related objective markers; the initial motorcycle is deliberately limited compared with later upgraded builds.

### 3.3.3 Bugged the Hell Out

**Region:** Cascades.

**Main purpose:** Scavenge medical supplies for Boozer after his injury.

**Tips:** Check buildings for useful items, manage Freakers quietly, and return to the safehouse when directed.

### 3.3.4 Chasing Leon and recovering supplies

**Regions:** Cascades / Belknap.

**Main purpose:** Follow the consequences of the opening chase and decide where to direct recovered supplies.

**Decision:** An early supply choice supports either Copeland's Camp or Tucker's Hot Springs. The choice affects immediate trust and credits, not a branching ending.

**Tips:** Copeland supports early motorcycle development; Hot Springs supports early weapon access. Both camps remain available during the campaign.

### 3.3.5 Boozer's worsening injury

**Regions:** Cascades / Belknap.

**Main purpose:** Help Boozer survive the consequences of the Ripper attack and establish the central conflict with the Rippers.

**Tips:** Keep healing supplies ready and prioritize the mission prompts. Some events require returning to the safehouse after another job or radio call.

### 3.3.6 Early NERO investigations

**Regions:** Cascades / Belknap.

**Main purpose:** Track NERO activity and discover clues connected to Sarah and the outbreak.

**Tips:** NERO surveillance missions often emphasize stealth and observation rather than open combat. Stay concealed and follow the marked researcher.

## 3.4 Lost Lake and Iron Butte

### 3.4.1 Arrival at Lost Lake

**Region:** Lost Lake.

**Characters:** Iron Mike, Rikki Patil, Addy Walker, Skizzo, Boozer.

**Main purpose:** Bring Boozer to safety, join the Lost Lake community, and build relationships with its leadership.

**Tips:** Visit the camp's merchants and mechanic early; Lost Lake becomes a central hub for mid-game progression.

### 3.4.2 Boozer's treatment and recovery

**Region:** Lost Lake.

**Main purpose:** Secure treatment for Boozer, help him adjust to his injury, and complete associated story missions.

**Tips:** Several of these are character-focused objectives rather than major combat missions. They are essential to the emotional story arc.

### 3.4.3 The Rippers and Carlos

**Regions:** Lost Lake / Iron Butte.

**Main purpose:** Investigate the Ripper threat, understand Carlos's connection to Deacon and Boozer, and confront the cult's leadership.

**Critical encounter:** The Carlos confrontation is a scripted melee-focused boss sequence. Use dodges, controlled strikes, and the combat prompts rather than relying on your normal firearms.

### 3.4.4 Skizzo's betrayal

**Regions:** Lost Lake / Iron Butte.

**Main purpose:** Survive the consequences of Skizzo's actions and escape hostile territory.

**Tips:** Equipment availability and mission constraints may change temporarily. Prioritize the scripted escape and recovery objectives.

### 3.4.5 Lost Lake relationships

**Region:** Lost Lake.

**Main purpose:** Continue Rikki, Addy, Iron Mike, and Boozer's storylines while preparing for the southern expedition.

**Tips:** Complete available camp jobs and upgrades before the story warns of a major regional departure. You can revisit the northern regions later.

## 3.5 Southern Regions — Crater Lake and Highway 97

### 3.5.1 Journey south

**Region:** Route toward Crater Lake.

**Main purpose:** Follow the new lead concerning Sarah and reach the Deschutes County Militia's territory.

**Tips:** Restock before leaving. The game temporarily restricts returning north during this stage.

### 3.5.2 Joining the militia

**Regions:** Crater Lake / Highway 97.

**Characters:** Colonel Matthew Garret, Captain Derrick Kouri, Sarah Whitaker, militia personnel.

**Main purpose:** Enter the militia, gain access to its facilities, and learn its leadership structure.

**Tips:** Diamond Lake is especially important for bike upgrades; Wizard Island is important for advanced weapons.

### 3.5.3 Finding Sarah

**Region:** Wizard Island, Crater Lake.

**Main purpose:** Reunite Deacon with Sarah and understand her role in ongoing scientific research.

**Tips:** Expect dialogue-heavy missions interwoven with supply runs and hostile-area expeditions.

### 3.5.4 Sarah's research missions

**Regions:** Crater Lake / Highway 97.

**Main purpose:** Help Sarah gather equipment and samples for her work on the Freaker infection.

**Tips:** Prepare for both human enemies and infected wildlife. Keep a versatile primary weapon and an effective special weapon.

### 3.5.5 The Reacher encounter

**Region:** Southern NERO research area.

**Main purpose:** Encounter the fast, powerful Reacher variant and learn more about the infection's evolution.

**Boss strategy:** Keep moving, use Focus to track the target, and punish its recovery windows. A Reacher can close distance much faster than a typical Swarmer.

### 3.5.6 Chemult and the first major mandatory horde

**Region:** Highway 97 / Chemult area.

**Main purpose:** Clear a large horde as part of the militia-era campaign.

**Tips:** Stock up on napalm Molotovs and other explosives. Scout fuel trucks, narrow passages, and retreat routes. Open with explosives against clustered Freakers and use automatic fire to finish survivors.

### 3.5.7 The militia's escalating conflict

**Regions:** Crater Lake / Highway 97.

**Main purpose:** Discover Garret's increasingly extreme plans and the threat they pose to civilian settlements.

**Tips:** Finish any immediately available southern camp upgrades and jobs before the return north.

## 3.6 Return North

### 3.6.1 Reaching the northern settlements

**Regions:** Lost Lake / Iron Butte / Cascades / Belknap.

**Main purpose:** Warn allies, reunite with surviving friends, and prepare for the militia's advance.

**Tips:** Previously restricted northern areas become available again. This is a good time to finish NERO sites, infestations, and camp trust goals.

### 3.6.2 Preparing the counterattack

**Region:** Lost Lake.

**Main purpose:** Coordinate the response to the militia and prepare for the final assault.

**Tips:** Refill ammunition, repair the bike, and choose weapons that work against armored human enemies and large Freaker groups.

## 3.7 Finale — Wizard Island

### 3.7.1 The final assault

**Region:** Crater Lake / Wizard Island.

**Main purpose:** Attack the militia's stronghold, confront Skizzo, and resolve the conflict with Colonel Garret.

**Tips:** Expect extended human combat, scripted sequences, and limited opportunities to restock. Use cover, explosives, and accurate headshots against dangerous enemies.

### 3.7.2 Story resolution

**Main purpose:** Resolve Deacon and Sarah's central story and the immediate fate of the militia. The conclusion does not eliminate the larger global Freaker threat.

## 3.8 Epilogue and Post-Credits Missions

Continue playing after the credits. Several storylines conclude through subsequent radio calls and missions, including a significant late O'Brian revelation connected to the infection. Do not stop at the credits if your goal is to see all narrative endings.

# 4. Side Missions Guide

## 4.1 Mission Types and Tracking

Side content is split between **camp jobs** (often initiated by radio calls), **storyline-linked missions**, and **world activities** such as camps, infestations, NERO sites, and hordes. The map and Storylines menu display the relevant objective and progression percentage. A camp job may contribute to more than one storyline.

### 4.1.1 How camp jobs unlock

- Advance the main story and build access to the camp.
- Wait for a call from the camp leader or job giver.
- Travel to the camp or designated start marker when prompted.
- Complete the tracking, rescue, bounty, or elimination objective.
- Return or report when required to collect credits and trust.

**Important:** Camp job availability is gated by story progress and sometimes by finishing an earlier job. The full set does not appear immediately after first entering a camp.

## 4.2 Copeland's Camp — Cascades

**Camp leader:** Mark Copeland.  
**Camp specialty:** Early motorcycle upgrades.  
**Job themes:** Tracking drifters, investigating attacks, locating missing people, and eliminating nearby threats.

### 4.2.1 Recommended approach

1. Take available radio jobs as you explore Cascades.
2. Clear nearby ambush camps to expose roads and useful locations.
3. Send rescued survivors to Copeland if you want to prioritize bike trust.
4. Purchase fuel and performance improvements before long northern trips.

### 4.2.2 Rewards

Camp credits, Copeland trust, and progress in associated storylines. Exact amounts vary by job and should be read from the in-game mission reward screen.

## 4.3 Hot Springs — Belknap

**Camp leader:** Ada “Tucker” Tucker.  
**Camp specialty:** Early firearms.  
**Job themes:** Bounties, missing workers, raiders, and local threats.

### 4.3.1 Recommended approach

Prioritize Hot Springs if you need stronger early firearms. Visit the merchant to compare trust-locked weapons. Bring sufficient ammunition when pursuing armed targets; bounty missions can escalate into camp-wide firefights.

### 4.3.2 Rewards

Hot Springs credits, trust, and storyline progress. Survivors sent to Hot Springs contribute to that camp's progression according to the rescue reward option offered.

## 4.4 Lost Lake — Lost Lake Region

**Camp leader:** Iron Mike.  
**Major contacts:** Rikki Patil, Addy Walker, Skizzo, Boozer.  
**Camp specialty:** Mid-game equipment, firearms, and motorcycle development.

### 4.4.1 Job themes

**Key contact:** Captain Derrick Kouri.  
**Camp specialty:** Advanced motorcycle upgrades.

## 4.6 Wizard Island — Crater Lake Region

**Leadership:** Colonel Matthew Garret and the Deschutes County Militia.  
**Camp specialty:** Advanced weapons.

### 4.6.1 Job themes

Bounty targets, dangerous raider camps, and militia security operations. Main-story science missions are separate from ordinary optional camp jobs even when they begin at Wizard Island.

### 4.6.2 Recommended approach

Invest in advanced firearms and ammunition capacity before the largest hordes. Complete available jobs while the southern region is open, then revisit unfinished content when the story allows.

## 4.7 Bounty Hunter Storyline

### 4.7.1 Objective

Track and neutralize wanted human targets for the camps. Bounty targets may flee on foot or by motorcycle, hide among hostile groups, or require proof of elimination.

### 4.7.2 Procedure

1. Accept the job from the relevant camp.
2. Inspect the indicated location and follow clues when necessary.
3. Mark enemy positions and identify the bounty target.
4. Stop escape attempts; preserve your own bike if a chase begins.
5. Collect required evidence and finish the mission's reporting step.

### 4.7.3 Rewards

Camp trust, camp credits, and progress toward the Bounty Hunter storyline completion percentage.

## 4.8 Marauder Camp Hunter Storyline

### 4.8.1 Objective

Eliminate marauder groups tied to missions and protect civilians or settlements from human threats. These encounters differ from the **14 Ambush Camp Hunter** world camps: marauder missions are generally job/storyline driven.

### 4.8.2 Procedure

Scout camps from cover, eliminate snipers first, disable isolated enemies quietly, and check for traps. Some camps have rescue objectives or named leaders.

### 4.8.3 Rewards

### 4.9.1 Objective

Rescue or protect civilians endangered by Freakers, raiders, or hostile drifters. Some missions involve tracking a missing person, entering an occupied building, or freeing a captive.

### 4.9.2 Procedure

Avoid indiscriminate explosives near captives. Clear hostile enemies before approaching the rescue marker, and follow any escort or interaction prompt.

### 4.9.3 Rewards

Storyline progress and applicable camp trust or credits. Some survivor encounters allow a choice of destination camp.

## 4.10 Infestation Exterminator Storyline

2. Identify each nest entrance or infected structure.

4. Prepare for Freakers emerging after the nest burns.

Experience, progress toward the Infestation Exterminator storyline, and improved access through affected areas. Detailed zone-by-zone counts are reserved for Section 5.

Crafting recipes, experience, and storyline completion progress. Section 6 covers the individual camps and bunker locations.

## 8.8 Reliable horde tactics

### 8.8.1 Early game

Start with Horse Lake, O'Leary Mountain and Little Bear Lake. Carry Molotovs, a primary automatic weapon, bandages, and stamina consumables. Keep the bike pointed away from the encounter and never run into a dead-end cave.

### 8.8.2 Midgame

Use attractors to gather Freakers, then explosives to damage concentrated groups. Place proximity mines near narrow exits and make long loops through obstacles. A suppressor is useful for approaching, but loud sustained fire is expected once the horde attacks.

### 8.8.3 Endgame

High-capacity weapons such as the Chicago Chopper, RPD or MG 55 are strong options. Use Focus to land headshots during short windows, sprint to create distance, then fire into the densest section of the group. The Old Sawmill has explosive props and constricted routes; rehearse a path before beginning.

## 9.2 Regional collectible sweep

### 9.2.1 Cascades

Begin at O'Leary Mountain and Copeland's Camp, then sweep Pioneer Cemetery, Horse Lake, Little Bear Lake, the logging areas and radio tower. ### 9.2.2 Belknap

Sweep Hot Springs, Crazy Willie's, Marion Forks, Patjens Lakes, Black Crater and the tunnel checkpoints. Tourism collectibles commonly appear in settlements and visitor locations; Ripper broadcasts appear in their controlled territory.

### 9.2.3 Lost Lake

Sweep Iron Mike's camp, Sherman's Camp, Rogue Camp, Berley Lake, the Sawmill and Santiam Tunnel. Listen for the six guitarist performances in the camp's evening social area. Revisit the camp on separate evenings until the collectible counter shows all six.

### 9.2.4 Iron Butte

### 9.2.5 Crater Lake

Sweep Diamond Lake, Wizard Island, Rimview Ranch, Tumblebug River, Spruce Lake and the volcanic scenic byway. Pay special attention to militia-related notes and speeches before advancing the finale.

### 9.2.6 Highway 97

Sweep Chemult, Chemult Community College, the rail line, Aspen Butte, Bare Bay, Pillette Bridge and northern mountain caves. Combine NERO research-site collection with horde reconnaissance.

## 9.3 IPCA Tech — secret weapon

Collect **18 IPCA Tech** components from NERO-related sites and corpses, then complete the necessary late-game/post-story NERO storyline. The components are used to assemble the **IPCA Stun Gun**, a *Syphon Filter* reference. The weapon's availability is tied to the secret NERO storyline progression; merely finding the components early does not immediately grant the completed weapon. Inspect hazmat-suited NERO bodies near research locations.

## 9.4 Anarchist Cairns

There are **12 Anarchist Cairns** for the *Go Kick Rocks* trophy. They are small stacked-rock formations, often along remote paths, hillsides and rocky trails. Approach and kick them over. Cairns are not part of the 240 standard collectible tally. Check off all twelve as the trophy tracks cumulative destruction, not a single region's map completion.

## 10.3 Camp-by-camp progression

### 10.3.1 Copeland's Camp

An early source of bike improvements. Prioritize fuel capacity, engine, and any affordable handling upgrades. Camp trust unlocks better stock; credits are local to the encampment.

### 10.3.2 Hot Springs

Tucker's Hot Springs primarily emphasizes firearms rather than the broad motorcycle upgrade progression. Direct bike-upgrade investment toward Copeland, Iron Mike's, and Diamond Lake when possible.

### 10.3.3 Iron Mike's Camp

Provides substantial midgame bike improvements. Upgrade fuel tank and saddlebags to support long routes and horde hunting. Balance bike spending against trust and available camp credits.

### 10.3.4 Diamond Lake

The strongest late-game bike upgrade source. Higher engine, nitrous, fuel, and durability tiers make long-distance exploration and jump-access NERO sites easier. Focus trust here for the motorcycle build.

### 10.3.5 Wizard Island

Primarily a major late-game weapon supplier rather than the main destination for high-tier bike performance parts. Use the southern camps strategically: Diamond Lake for bike upgrades, Wizard Island for guns.

## 10.4 Trust levels and credits

Most meaningful upgrades are gated by **Trust Level 1, 2, or 3** and a camp-specific credit cost. Exact prices vary by upgrade tier, and the store inventory provides the authoritative price and unlock requirement. Earn trust through camp jobs, bounties, rescued survivors, and local horde/infestation activities. A survivor rescue can be directed to the camp whose trust you most need.

## 10.5 Visual customization

Mechanics offer combinations of paint, decals, and accent styling. Some custom designs are unlocked through storylines, challenges, or edition-specific bonuses. Cosmetic choices do not replace performance upgrades. PlayStation crossover decals reference other games and franchises; their availability may depend on game edition and update.

## 10.6 Suggested purchase order

1. First meaningful fuel tank upgrade.
2. Engine upgrade for safer travel and pursuit escapes.
3. Saddlebags for ammunition replenishment.
4. Frame/durability for rough terrain.
5. Nitrous for distant jumps and NERO research sites.
6. Tires, suspension, and exhaust.
7. Visual paint, decals and accent upgrades.

### 11.0.6 Suppressors, ammunition, and explosives

Suppressors are consumable attachments for compatible firearms. Their condition degrades when fired, so remove them when stealth is no longer necessary. Suppressors can be purchased at merchants and sometimes recovered from vehicle engine compartments. Ammunition can be bought from merchants or gun lockers, looted from defeated enemies, and found in police-car trunks and supply boxes. Molotov cocktails are essential for destroying Freaker infestation nests. Other offensive tools include grenades, pipe bombs, proximity bombs, remote bombs, and attractors; their availability depends on exploration, merchant progression, and crafting-recipe unlocks.

Camp trust and credits gate merchant inventories. Horde Killer storyline milestones unlock additional firearms.

## 12.2 Human factions

### 12.2.1 Marauders

Bandits use firearms, traps, roadblocks, sniper nests and ambush tactics. Scout camps with binoculars and prioritize snipers. Roadside wires can throw Deacon from the bike. Marauder camp clearing is separate from ordinary ambush camp clearing.

### 12.2.2 Rippers

The Rest in Peace cult follows Carlos and practices extreme violence and ritual scarring. Rippers may behave unpredictably around Freakers due to their ideology. Use stealth against isolated patrols and watch for traps and fire-based weapons.

### 12.2.3 Anarchists

Hostile survivors associated with Anarchist camps and the mine storyline. Their environmental markings and rock cairns connect to optional exploration. Clear human enemies before triggering nearby Freaker groups.

### 12.2.4 Deschutes County Militia

Organized and well-equipped soldiers under Colonel Garret. Expect automatic weapons, armor and guarded installations. Suppressed shots and positional advantages are especially useful against larger militia formations.

## 12.3 Uninfected and infected wildlife

Ordinary deer and wolves are sources of meat and survival encounters. Bears are formidable even without infection. **Runners** (infected wolves) and **Ragers** (infected bears) are especially dangerous during travel. Use the bike's maneuverability to avoid being surrounded; do not stop next to a pack unless prepared.

## 12.4 Enemy-specific combat rules

1. **Screamers:** kill before they summon extra Freakers.
2. **Breakers:** do not trade melee blows; dodge and attack during recovery windows.
3. **Reachers:** watch for sudden bursts of speed and reposition constantly.
4. **Ragers:** avoid being trapped against rocks or buildings.
5. **Newts:** scan rooftops, especially in abandoned settlements.
6. **Hordes:** prioritize escape routes and crowd control over isolated kills.

## 13.2 Deacon St. John

Deacon is a former member of the Mongrels Motorcycle Club who survives by bounty hunting and scavenging. He believes Sarah died during the outbreak but continues searching for evidence. His relationships with Boozer, Sarah, and the competing camps anchor the story. His combat style emphasizes mobility, improvisation and familiarity with the Oregon backcountry.

## 13.3 Boozer

Boozer is badly burned by Rippers early in the game, making his recovery a major narrative thread. Deacon's decision to seek help at Lost Lake shifts the campaign toward a more settled community. Boozer's role evolves from injured companion to a key figure in the final assault.

## 13.4 Sarah Whitaker

Sarah is a scientist whose work is connected to the virus's origin and potential countermeasures. Deacon's search for her drives the transition from the northern camps to the militia-controlled south. Their reunion creates conflict between personal loyalty and the militia's priorities.

## 13.5 Iron Mike, Rikki and Addy

Iron Mike seeks a community built on cooperation rather than constant retaliation. Rikki is essential to the camp's mechanical and engineering work, including power infrastructure. Addy provides medical care and supports Boozer's recovery. Their differing priorities make Lost Lake the story's central civilian settlement.

## 13.6 Skizzo and Carlos

Skizzo is driven by suspicion and self-preservation and becomes a principal human antagonist. Carlos is the cult leader of the Rippers, with a history connected to Deacon and Boozer. Their arcs illustrate different ways the collapse reshaped personal loyalties.

## 13.7 Copeland and Tucker

Copeland runs a camp that prizes independence, self-reliance and his conspiratorial broadcasts. Tucker runs Hot Springs with a harsh labor system. Both offer jobs and upgrades, but each encampment represents a different approach to survival.

## 13.8 Colonel Garret, Kouri and O'Brian

Garret leads a religiously motivated militia campaign and controls Wizard Island. Kouri is a pragmatic officer whose choices affect Deacon's fate. O'Brian's investigations and eventual revelation provide the game's most consequential post-credits twist.

## 14.5 Efficient platinum route

1. Complete the main story, clearing camps and NERO sites when convenient.
2. Earn trust through jobs, survivor rescues, and regional activities.
3. Collect at least 181 of the 240 standard collectibles.
4. Finish the 14 ambush camps, 10 Marauder camps, infestation storyline, and 30 NERO locations.
5. Max a stat with injectors, earn all 45 skills, and complete motorcycle and crafting trophies.
6. Complete any remaining horde-related objectives, knife kill, drift/nitrous and other combat counters.
7. Finish separate Survival, New Game+ and Challenge Mode trophy sets if aiming for all DLC/post-launch trophies.

# 15. Easter Eggs and Secrets

## 15.1 Secret NERO ending

After the main story, continue completing post-credit storylines and wait for the O'Brian-related follow-up. The encounter reveals that O'Brian has changed in ways that dramatically expand the implications of the Freaker virus. This is a narrative epilogue, not a separate alternate campaign ending. The scene is one of the game's major sequel hooks.

## 15.2 Syphon Filter connections

Bend Studio previously developed the *Syphon Filter* series. The **IPCA Stun Gun** is a deliberate connection to that franchise. Its assembly requires IPCA Tech and relevant storyline progress. The **Logan's Shadow** trophy name also references *Syphon Filter* protagonist Gabriel Logan.

## 15.3 PlayStation crossover bike designs

Depending on edition and available updates, cosmetic bike decals and tank designs reference other PlayStation-associated games, including **God of War**, **Horizon Zero Dawn**, and **Death Stranding**. These are visual rewards or promotional cosmetics, not additional story missions or equipment-stat bonuses.

## 15.4 The 989-ear reference

The trophy **Lend Me Your Ears** requires 989 Freaker ears. The number references **989 Studios**, a publisher associated with the early *Syphon Filter* games. Freaker ears are also a practical camp-bounty currency.

## 15.5 Environmental storytelling

Abandoned NERO checkpoints, memorial sites, roadblocks, refugee facilities, and Ripper sermons provide context for the collapse. Audio recorders, tourist materials, and character notes are valuable for reconstructing the timeline. Listen to radio calls and NERO recordings rather than treating them only as collectible counters.

## 15.6 Optional discoveries worth revisiting

- Revisit O'Leary Mountain after important Boozer story events.
- Return to Lost Lake after the finale for character dialogue and camp changes.
- Finish the NERO storyline after the credits for the O'Brian revelation.
- Collect IPCA Tech for the special weapon.
- Inspect militia locations for speeches and notes.
- Revisit early areas with advanced nitrous to reach isolated research sites.
